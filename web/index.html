The proof pays the filler.

Medieval tally halves, Winchester City Council Museums.

Notched, then split

A medieval tally was notched with the amount owed, then split lengthwise. Each party kept one half, and the debt was settled only when the two halves fit.

Two halves, two chains

In Talyx the order on Sepolia is one half and the fill on V3 is the other. The FillBook contract records who filled, for whom and how much, in the same call that pays.

Checked, not held

Nobody holds the halves together. The escrow checks V3’s signed proof that the fill exists, byte for byte, and pays only if every byte matches.

Fair questions

What do I still have to trust?

V3’s two BEEFY keys, which are probably development keys, and V3’s sudo key. Not a relayer, and not a committee. Talyx is testnet-only and unaudited.

What if nobody fills my order?

After the deadline plus a 30-minute claim window, you call refund and your ETH comes back. Claim and refund can never both happen.

Can the relayer steal or redirect the payment?

No. The claim pays the payee recorded on V3. A relayer can delay a claim; it cannot change who is paid.

Who are the fillers?

Anyone holding tPOT on V3. The filler script watches for new orders and fills them, and a filler earns the spread between the ETH escrowed and the tPOT sent.

What if the fill landed before the latest signed block?

State is cumulative. Any later signed block still holds the fill record, so the proof only needs the newest leaf of that block’s MMR.

Why must my amount divide by 10⁴?

tPOT has 14 decimals and EVM contracts count in 18. Anything finer than 10⁴ EVM units cannot exist on V3, so the escrow refuses it instead of rounding your tPOT into dust.

Why only onto V3, not back out?

Proofs flow out of V3, which is enough to protect you on the way in. Paying out of V3 would need a light client of the other chain running on V3; that direction is planned as Bonded Fast Exit.

Why does this need Portaldot V3?

V3 runs EVM contracts through Revive and signs its own BEEFY finality with plain secp256k1 keys that Solidity can check. Portaldot mainnet (spec 1002) has neither yet.
